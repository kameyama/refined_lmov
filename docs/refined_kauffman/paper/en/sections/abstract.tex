\begin{abstract}
We refine the large $N$ duality for the colored Kauffman invariant of $\SO(N)$ Chern--Simons theory \cite{Marino09} within the orientifold refined topological string \cite{AgSc12}. We define a refined crosscap invariant $g = f - \tfrac12 f^{\cov}$ from the $\SO(2N)$ refined Chern--Simons invariants, assembled with the dual Cauchy kernel of Koornwinder polynomials, and those of the $\U(2N-1)$ cover. Assuming a classification of one-particle states modelled on \cite{AgSc12}, we derive from the M-theory index the structure $g = \tfrac12 E/(1+t) + C$, with $E$ a signed count of states invariant under the orientifold involution and $C$ a count of frozen states. From DAHA-Jones polynomials at generic $t$, by interpolation in $N$, we obtain $g$ at general $N$ for two-strand torus knots with up to three boxes (four for the trefoil), for $T(3,4)$ in the fundamental representation and, through the iterated DAHA-Jones polynomials of \cite{CD14}, for two algebraic cables of the trefoil. In these cases the $N$-dependent poles cancel, $g$ is an integer Laurent polynomial up to the factor $2(1+t)$ predicted by the index in even sectors, and its refined part is divisible by $(\bt^2 - 1)(\ba^2 - \bq^2)$. For the fundamental representation of $T(2,2m+1)$, $m \le 5$, the $c = 1$ part has a $G$-expansion whose coefficients have a common sign in each block up to the factor $(\bt^2-1)$, and the $c = 2$ part is $-(\ba^2 - \bq^2)(\bt^2 - 1)$ times a positive polynomial; we conjecture this sign property for all $m$, which fails for $T(3,4)$ and the cables. The brane--brane sector of the cover reproduces the refined LMOV invariants of \cite{KN17} for the trefoil and $T(2,5)$ with up to two boxes, and we conjecture a closed form of the brane--image sector of two-strand torus knots as a space of states, checked for $T(2,3)$--$T(2,15)$. For $T(2,3)$ and $T(2,5)$ the refined $\Sp(2N)$ invariants of \cite{AM22} satisfy $g^{\Sp}(\ba) = -g^{\SO}(-\ba)$ in the fundamental representation. For higher representations the signs do not align; we conjecture that the obstruction lies in the multiplet structure of the frozen states.
\end{abstract}
