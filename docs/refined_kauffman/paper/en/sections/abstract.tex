\begin{abstract}
We refine the large $N$ duality for the colored Kauffman invariant of $\SO(N)$ Chern--Simons theory \cite{Marino09} in the framework of the orientifold refined topological string \cite{AgSc12}. A refined crosscap invariant $g = f - \tfrac12 f^{\cov}$ is defined from the $\SO(2N)$ refined Chern--Simons invariants, assembled with the dual Cauchy kernel of Koornwinder polynomials, minus one half of the corresponding free energy of the $\U(2N-1)$ cover. From the M-theory index of the orientifold we derive, under a classification of one-particle states modelled on \cite{AgSc12}, the structure $g = \tfrac12 E/(1+t) + C$ with $E$ a signed count of states invariant under the orientifold involution and $C$ a count of frozen states, together with the boundary factor of $E$. Using DAHA-Jones polynomials at generic $t$ we compute $g$ at general $N$ for torus knots with up to four boxes and, through the iterated DAHA-Jones polynomials of \cite{CD14}, for two algebraic cables of the trefoil. In all cases the $N$-dependent poles cancel, $g$ is an integer Laurent polynomial up to the factor $2(1+t)$ predicted by the index in even sectors, and its refined part is divisible by the universal factor $(\bt^2 - 1)(\ba^2 - \bq^2)$. For the fundamental representation of two-strand torus knots the $c = 1$ part admits a $G$-expansion whose coefficients have, block by block in the K\"ahler variable, a common sign up to the factor $(\bt^2-1)$, and the $c = 2$ part is $-(\ba^2 - \bq^2)(\bt^2 - 1)$ times a positive polynomial; this sign property fails for $T(3,4)$ and for the cables. Splitting the cover into brane and image, the brane--brane sector reproduces the refined LMOV invariants of \cite{KN17}, and for two-strand torus knots the brane--image sector is identified as a space of states. The refined $\Sp(2N)$ invariants of \cite{AM22} satisfy $g^{\Sp}(\ba) = -g^{\SO}(-\ba)$ in the fundamental representation. For higher representations the signs do not align; we conjecture that the obstruction lies in the multiplet structure of the frozen states.
\end{abstract}
